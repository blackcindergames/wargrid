## Findings (short, cited)

Today's bot and the bar:

- **source, Wargrid probe** (private notes) Today's bot against itself, 45 matches on four maps: a seat never damaged an enemy building in 30 of 90 seat rows; 15 matches had no winner at the 25-minute cap, the seats holding a median of 106 credits; a barracks or factory stood with an empty queue in 61 % of samples, and in only 4 % while its cheapest unit could be paid for, so the bot is short of money, not of things to buy; it issues about 18 orders a minute, 42 in its busiest minute. How it does against a person has never been measured.
- **not found** A human-skill bar for the top honest level of any shipped real-time strategy game read. **source** One shipped turn-based strategy game measured its top bot on its live ranked ladder, unannounced: "within the top 25% of human players on the Prismata rank ladder" (Churchill and Buro, Game AI Pro 3, ch. 30, section 30.5). **source** When Age of Empires IV gave its Hardest level doubled gathering, players objected to the step: the boost "created too much of a step-up in difficulty from Hard to Hardest" and "more difficulty options would be preferable", so the boost moved to three new levels above it (patch 6.1.130, ageofempires.com). **source** Where developers say who a level is for, they use one sentence per level: Zero-K's lobby runs from "no strategy game experience" to veterans of the game itself, and its Hard reads "Recommended for veteran strategy gamers who aren't afraid of losing." (Spring-Chobby/Chobby @ 889999a `LuaMenu/configs/gameConfig/zk/aiSimpleName.lua:66-73`).
- **source** StarCraft II has seven honest levels and three levels whose slot name says "Cheater" with the cheat in the tooltip (SC2 data strings `:5040-5070`). One routine switches behaviours on by level (retreat from Hard, backing off when damaged and extra scouts from Harder, kiting from Very Hard), each level up to Very Hard has an action cap (Elite runs without one), and the opening is a weighted random roll per match. But its five lower levels run a separate scripted build system with forced gaps between worker builds, which Wargrid's rule forbids, so it is no proof that removal alone makes a middle level (SC2 data `BaseAI.galaxy:99-156`, `MeleeLowAI.galaxy:18-25`, `:235-306`, `MeleeBuildAI.galaxy:319-404`).
- **source** Command & Conquer 3 and Red Alert 3 have a difficulty setting next to a personality setting. Red Alert 3 lists per level which behaviours may run: its easy bot does not expand or detect stealth, and only hard and brutal flank; the lower levels also get their own weaker attack and defence behaviours. Brutal is hard with doubled income and its own openings (CnC SDK `Red Alert 3/Xml/SkirmishAI/Personalities/AlliedCoopBaseSkirmishPersonality.xml:13-76`, `CoopBasePersonality.xml:23-24`).
- **source** Zero Hour has three levels, easy, normal and hard (ZH src `Include/Common/GameCommon.h:141-148`). Its skirmish scripts are fixed per side and level; the only random element the decode records is a 0 to 2 s offset on when a script is re-checked (`docs/reference/m7-verification-archive-research.md:83`).
- **source** (medians and means computed for this file from the SkillCraft and GGTracker files named above) What separates human skill bands at every step is how fast a player looks somewhere and acts: Bronze players act at a new place about 12 times a minute and need about 1.05 s before the first action there, Platinum 18 times and 0.71 s, professionals 28 times and 0.40 s. The amount done per look is about the same at every band. Weaker players also leave more money unspent for the income they have (a Gold player about 1.5 times a Master player), and the gap is wider in longer matches.

Unit rules:

- **source, Wargrid** A unit's automatic behaviour depends on whether its owner is a bot seat. An idle bot-owned unit looks for enemies within `max(vision x 1.333, weapon reach)` and walks to them. An idle human-owned unit looks only within weapon reach and does not walk; it uses `vision x 2.2` only after an attack-move target is lost. Dropping a target that left weapon reach, that re-scan and retaliation apply to human-owned units only (`combat.gd` `is_ai` :73, `scan_radius` :77, the planning loop :113-122, `_scan_radius_for` :170, and the `is_ai` tests in `object_removed` :620 and `_retaliate_trigger` :649).
- **source** Zero Hour has the same split. A human-owned unit that acquires a target by itself only takes targets already within attack range and may not pursue; a computer-owned unit pursues, and only computer-owned units have moods: a code comment says human units have no mood (`docs/reference/m5.1-acquisition.md`; ZH src `GameLogic/Object/Update/AIUpdate.cpp:4328-4345`, `:4625-4631`, `GameLogic/AI/AIStates.cpp:2612-2625`). When a squad attacks a squad, Zero Hour's easy bots pick a random enemy, normal the closest, hard all the same one, and units ordered by a human get the hard rule (`GameLogic/AI/AIStates.cpp:5928-6019`).
- **source, Wargrid** Offline bot seats use the bot rules (`match/offline_driver.gd` `create` passes "ai" to `skirmish.gd` `create`, whose `second_controller` also defaults to it; `debug/team_match.gd` `create`). Online and headless seats are created as human seats, so a bot behind an online seat already plays with the human rules (`docs/guide/09-ai.md`, "The bot behind a lockstep seat"). P7 brings host-run bots into online matches and has no design yet.
- **source, Wargrid probe** (private notes) The same bot and faction on both seats, four maps: with one seat on bot rules and one on human rules, the bot-rules seat won 23 matches, the human-rules seat 20, and 33 of 76 had no winner at the 25-minute cap. The rule set changes single results a lot (the Ashfall match of the 2026-11-15 launch report ends at tick 26,430 with mixed rules and has no winner with bot rules on both seats) and shows no clear gain in strength on average; the human-rules seats reached an enemy building later (median tick 15,357 against 12,241).

Human feel:

- **source, Wargrid** Every offline match starts the simulation's random numbers from the same value (`simulation.gd` `RNG_SEED`), and the bot uses no randomness, so the bot opens the same way every match and first attacks at the same tick. A match seed already exists and every client gets it at the start, but it only picks start spots (`match_settings.gd` `starts`, `net/wire.gd`).
- **source** The Maia chess project found that a weakened strong engine does not play like a weaker human: depth-limited Stockfish matched 33 to 41 % of human moves, and every depth matched stronger players better than weaker ones; a model built from players of one band matched 50 to 53 % at its best band (McIlroy-Young and others, KDD 2020, arXiv 2006.01855, section 4.2 and Table 1). Its authors note that their models "play the same move every time" and were given opening books (CSSLab/maia-chess @ 749204c, `README.md:39`, `:51`, `images/sf_lineplot.png`, `images/maia_lineplot.png`). **not found** A real-time strategy bot that plays like a human of a chosen skill.
- **source** In a study of two bots that received only what was in their field of view, players still believed the bots saw through walls, because the bots noticed enemies faster and more reliably than a person does (paper kept in schrum2/MM-NEAT @ 5ca8e9e, `batch/Experiments-2019-CoG-UT2004/friedman.cog19.pdf`, sections IV-C and V).
- **source** One study of people judging real-time strategy bots: 45 StarCraft players against 18 tournament bots; the human ranking had almost nothing to do with the tournament ranking (rank correlation 0.018). For human likeness the top comments were "Worse Decision Making than human players" and "Unit control style is different"; experts saw the bots keep their plan after scouting, and medium players rated the bots lowest (Kim, Kim, Kim, Dey, IEEE Access 6, 2018, Tables 8 and 9, section V).
- **source** What observers named as give-aways outside real-time strategy: in a blind test of 2D game players, jitter, useless moves, no long-term plan and too-fast reactions, and some observers thought one agent with superhuman reflexes was cheating (Khalifa and others, IJCAI 2016, section 6, Table 2); in BotPrize, firing at once on first sight and too-good aim, and "Our bot was too skilled" (the UT^2 team's BotPrize slides of 2010 and 2011, read in web archive copies).
- **source, Wargrid** Wargrid has no replay viewer for players; a match can only be replayed headless (`launcher.gd:22`, `--replay-log`).

The allied bot:

- **source, Wargrid** Today's bot defends only its own buildings: an intruder counts when it is within 10 cells of a building the seat owns (`skirmish.gd` `_defend`, `DEFENCE_RADIUS`).
- **source** Zero Hour has no order from a human to an allied computer player and no transfer of money or units between players. It has beacons, three per player by the code's default, which its bots ignore (ZH src `Include/Common/MessageStream.h:486-608`, `GameLogic/System/GameLogicDispatch.cpp:1606-1697`, `Common/MultiplayerSettings.cpp:74`). **not found** Whether a Zero Hour skirmish bot helps an allied base: the scripts decide it, and the decode in `docs/reference/m7-verification-archive-research.md` records no such rule.
- **source** StarCraft II lets a player place beacons for an allied bot (attack or guard here, follow this unit, scout here, expand here, and five army modes), and the bot reports back with fixed alert lines and short pings, among them a call for help defending (SC2 data strings `:1782-1855`).
- **source** 0 A.D. has one request, "attack this enemy", and its bot answers join, decline or "other target first" (0 A.D. `gui/session/diplomacy/playercontrols/AttackRequestButton.js:1-38`, `simulation/ai/petra/attackManager.js:58-113`). Two Warzone 2100 bots send their nearest army group to an ally's beacon for a bounded time and answer in one fixed line (WZ `nb_includes/chat.js:13-215`, `nexus_includes/help.js:7-216`). The Sorian bot code in Forged Alliance treats a human's ordinary map pings as its orders (FAForever/fa @ 806cb6d `lua/AI/sorianutilities.lua:643-672`).
- **source** One measured result on bot teammates, from a shooter: 30 players were split between a skilled, self-directed bot and a follower (17 to 13, not significant); what predicted the choice was the bot they rated more helpful and saw more often, not the one that scored more; five said the skilled ally was too good or ignored them (same paper as above, sections IV-B and IV-C).
- **source** Shipped request features read in the second round: Company of Heroes 3's bots answer pings (Hot Fix 1.0.7 notes, 2023: attack and defend pings "may" draw a response); StarCraft II's allied bots reported what they do one patch before players could order them (patch notes 2.0.4 and 2.0.5, 2013); Age of Empires II's allied computer players take numbered commands, among them "Give me your extra resources" (The Conquerors manual, 2000, pp. 9-10).
- **inferred** An allied bot's attack target and a call for help can be shown on every client without a new command type, because the bot's attack order and the attack on its base are things every client already sees. A request from a human to a bot needs a new command type.

End-game income:

## Questions

Q1. [answered a] Before any bot work, do you and one friend each play two short logged matches against today's bot?
   a) Yes, with the playtest helper, and after each match one tap: too easy, close or too hard   b) No, the first human data comes with M8
   Recommendation: a, because nobody has measured how today's bot does against a person, and every later bar needs that first number. Why it matters: it tells whether your group needs an easier bot, a harder one, or both; the same records give your own orders per minute, which the bot's pace should be set against. No ZH default.

Q2. [overtaken] Once your best player knows the bot, from about their fifth match on, what should the top level do against them? (Revisits the 2026-11-05 answer, because the 2026-11-17 wording differs.)
   a) Win most matches, about 7 of 10 (one reading of the 2026-11-05 answer "beat the user and friends regularly", which names no rate)   b) Win 3 to 7 of 10, with most matches close   c) Lose most, but only after a real fight
   Recommendation: b, because the 2026-11-17 wording is "challenging, not overwhelming", and a close match is what that feels like. Why it matters: it is the missing bar of the M8 gate. No ZH default: Zero Hour's hard level was never tied to a player.

Q3. [answered b] What should the middle level do against an average player of your group in their first matches?
   a) The player wins about 7 of 10   b) About even, with most matches close   c) The bot wins about 7 of 10
   Recommendation: b, because you expect to start on a middle level and work up, and "about even at first" leaves room in both directions. Why it matters: it places the middle of the ladder. No ZH default.

Q4. [answered b] How is that bar checked?
   a) By bot against bot (each level beats the one below) and one playtest at two or more levels with no bar (today's plan)   b) Also by about ten logged matches of your group at the middle and at the top level, each with the one tap of Q1   c) By logged matches at every level
   Recommendation: b, because bot against bot is cheap and repeatable but says nothing about people, and six levels times ten matches is more play than a group gives. Why it matters: ten matches cannot tell 5 of 10 from 7 of 10, so the tap ("close") carries as much as the score. No ZH default.

Q5. Should a bot improvement that passes its test ship before M8?
   a) No, all bot changes wait for M8, except a launch rule that wins both columns of the Bot attack test (today's plan)   b) Yes, as one small bot package once two or more have passed   c) Only fixes for matches that never end
   Recommendation: b, because you asked for quick, effective wins soon, and one package moves the recorded match traces once. Why it matters: P7 and P9 put today's bot in front of your friends long before M8. No ZH default.

Q6. [overtaken] Do you want to choose a bot level before the strong bot exists? (Revisits "strong bot first, ladder after", user, 2026-11-05, because you now expect to start on a middle level.)
   a) Yes, now: an easier bot beside today's, made by letting it decide less often — ZH default for having levels at all: ZH shipped three from the start (Include/Common/GameCommon.h:141-148)   b) No: one bot until M8.1, as decided on 2026-11-05   c) Decide after your matches of Q1 show which direction is needed
   Recommendation: c, because today's bot may already be too easy for you and too hard for a new friend, and only Q1 can tell. Why it matters: a level control costs a launcher change and moves the match traces.

Q7. [answered b] Until the M8 design, which automatic rules do a bot's units follow, offline and online? (Asked before the M8 design, where the 2026-11-11 answer put it, because P7 has to choose for online matches.)
   a) The bot rules everywhere: idle units notice enemies from further away and walk to them — ZH default for computer players (AIUpdate.cpp:4625-4631)   b) Your rules everywhere   c) As today: bot rules offline, your rules online
   Recommendation: b, because it is the plain reading of "same mechanics", the probe found no clear gain in strength from the bot rules, and then every playtest from P7 on is formed on rules the bot can keep. Why it matters: b moves the offline bot traces once and settles P7's online traces; a and c leave the one place where today's bot does not play by your rules. How your own units behave does not change with any option.

Q8. [answered b] From the M8 bot on, may a bot's units behave differently by themselves than yours? (The 2026-11-11 answer leaves this to the M8 design; answer now only if you want it settled early.)
   a) Yes, bot units get rules yours do not — ZH default (AIUpdate.cpp:4328-4345, AIStates.cpp:5964-5971)   b) No: one rule set, and where units should behave differently it is a stance you can set for your units and a bot sets by an ordinary order   c) No: one rule set and no stance; everything else is orders
   Recommendation: b, because the 2026-11-11 answer already leans toward a per-unit stance. Why it matters: with b you get a stance button for every unit, and M8's moods (Sleep, Alert, Aggressive) become that button's settings; with c they disappear.

Q9. Should a bot open and first attack the same way in every match?
   a) Yes, as today — ZH default: its scripts are fixed per side and level with only a 0 to 2 s random offset (m7-verification-archive-research.md:83)   b) No: at every level it picks one of two or three openings per faction at random each match, and its first attack time varies   c) Only the higher levels vary
   Recommendation: b, because a fixed opening and a first attack at the same second are the first things a player learns to exploit, and StarCraft II's bots roll a weighted opening per match (SC2 data MeleeBuildAI.galaxy:319-404). Why it matters: it is the cheapest step toward a bot that feels like a player; the draw comes from the match seed every client already has, so nothing can go out of step.

Q10. How do we test "feels human"?
   a) By automatic checks on the bot's recorded orders: variety between matches, pace, and the delay before it acts   b) By asking after each logged match: did it feel like playing a person (yes, partly, no), and what gave it away   c) Both
   Recommendation: c, because the checks are automatic and the question is the only one that asks a person; the give-aways are counted like the "units just stood there" remarks. Why it matters: today "feels human" has no definition, so nothing can fail it. No ZH default.

Q11. When your base is attacked, does your allied bot send help?
   a) No, as today (it defends only its own buildings)   b) Yes, from P7 on: its army at home comes   c) Yes, with the first later bot change
   Recommendation: b, because it is the one behaviour that most separates a teammate from a second army on your side. Why it matters: P7 was decided with today's bot (user, 2026-11-16), so b is an exception to approve: a few lines of bot logic, and the team match traces move once. ZH default: not found (the skirmish scripts decide it; the decode records no rule for helping an ally).

Q12. In P7, what should your allied bot tell you without being asked?
   a) Nothing, as today — ZH default (no message to or from a computer player, Include/Common/MessageStream.h:486-608)   b) Where it attacks: a mark on the map and one line of text when its army sets out   c) b, and a call when its base is under attack
   Recommendation: c, because both can be drawn on every screen from what the game already shows, so neither needs a new kind of order. Why it matters: it is the difference between a second army and a teammate; P7 needs a place for that one line, because the alert feed comes later.

Q13. When you attack, should your allied bot come along?
   a) No, it keeps its own timing, as today   b) It attacks the same enemy as you   c) It sets out when your army sets out   d) Both: it sets out with you, toward the enemy you attack
   Recommendation: d, with the later package for bot team play, because two armies arriving at different bases, or at different times, lose to one that arrives together. Why it matters: no bot read follows a human's army without a mark or a request, so this is Wargrid's own work. No ZH default.

Q14. With the later package for bot team play, should you be able to ask your allied bot for something?
   a) No — ZH default (no message to a computer player, Include/Common/MessageStream.h:486-608)   b) Yes, with the map ping you would give a human friend: the bot sends its next army there for a limited time and answers "coming" or "not ready" (Warzone 2100's Nexus, nexus_includes/help.js:7-216: 3 minutes plus travel; 0 A.D., attackManager.js:58-113: join or decline)   c) Yes, with a small menu: attack here, defend here, attack this enemy
   Recommendation: b, because you then learn one gesture for humans and bots, and the answer tells you whether the bot accepted. Why it matters: a request is a new kind of order that every client must replay the same way, which is why it is not P7 work.

Q15. The 2026-11-09 answer puts Supply Drop Zone, Internet Center and the Hacker's money ability into ground batch A; the blueprint says their place is not decided. Does the 2026-11-09 answer stand?
   a) Yes, and the blueprint is corrected   b) No, it stays open until ground batch A is planned   c) No, they get their own package before M8
   Recommendation: a, because it is the recorded answer. Why it matters: M8's economy package needs to know these exist before it; in the probe 15 of 45 matches had no winner at the cap, the seats at a median of 106 credits (highest 575), which a second income would change (inferred).
